TFX grade selection
TFX-DPC25P · SteelCVDP15–P35 + K10–K25

First choice in unalloyed and low-alloyed steel, and in grey and nodular cast iron. CVD stack carries the heat of a full-diameter cut.

TFX-DMV25M · StainlessPVDM10–M30 + P15–P25

Austenitic and duplex stainless, and steels that work-harden at the bore wall. PVD keeps the edge keen so the chip forms instead of smearing.

TFX-DUV25U · UniversalPVDP15–P30 + M15–M25

One grade across mixed batches. Choose it when the job list changes faster than the tool magazine.

Both inserts in the body carry the same grade. The peripheral insert does the outer diameter and takes the higher surface speed; the central insert works near zero cutting speed and fails by chipping rather than wear, which is why centre height matters more than grade at the middle of the hole.

Setting up — centre height

Mount the peripheral insert parallel to the machine X axis and facing the operator. On a lathe with upper and lower turrets, a drill in the lower turret still needs the peripheral insert toward the operator.

By design the central insert sits about 0.05 mm below centre. If the turret is offset from the spindle centreline that figure drifts, and the drill will either climb or leave a stub.

Check it, do not assume it:
1 · Drill a blind hole about 10 mm deep at fn below 0.1 mm/rev.
2 · Measure the core left standing at the bottom.
3 · Core about Ø0.5 mm → centre height is right.
4 · No core at all, or core over Ø1 mm → adjust.

A missing core is the dangerous case, not the reassuring one. With no core the carbide around the centre of the inner insert is unsupported and liable to collapse.
When it goes wrong
  1. Hole diameter drifts down the bore.Tapering usually means the chip is not clearing. Raise cutting speed and reduce feed. If the whole hole is undersize instead of tapered, the cause is plastic deformation of the workpiece, not the tool — move to a longer-series body.
  2. Can external coolant be used?No. Short-hole drilling produces too much chip for flood coolant to clear, and efficiency drops. Run internal coolant and keep the pressure up; edge life depends on it more than on any other single setting.
  3. Chips tangle instead of breaking.Sticky material and long chips respond to either low Vc + high fn, which makes a thick chip that breaks itself, or high Vc + low fn, which makes a thin chip that centrifugal force throws clear. If tangling only happens in the first 10 mm and settles after that, segment-cut 1–10 mm and run continuous from there.
  4. The tool chatters.Nearly always too little feed per revolution. If fn < 0.06 mm/rev, raise it to 0.08–0.12 mm/rev. If excessive speed is driving the vibration, drop to Vc ≈ 100–150 m/min. Visible burring at the drill, or a workpiece that is not clamped hard, calls for the opposite move — back the feed off to 0.07–0.08 mm/rev.
  5. Scored walls in the finished hole.Change the cutting parameters, or change the retract path so the insert is not dragging on the way out.
