Grade TFX-MHV20 — Coating Architecture
TFX-MHV20 PVD TiAlN H10–H25
PVD TiAlN coating on ultra-fine cemented carbide substrate, purpose-engineered for milling hardened steels and tool steels up to ~55 HRC. Under cutting heat TiAlN forms a stable aluminium-oxide surface film, keeping the coating hard at temperatures where TiAlN would already have oxidised — the property that makes hardened-steel milling possible at all. The sharp PVD edge minimises cutting forces on the brittle workpiece surface. Primary application is finish and semi-finish face milling and shoulder milling of die steels, tool steels, and through-hardened alloy steel.

Ultra-Fine WC-Co Substrate
Base
◈
TiAlN Low-Friction Surface
Friction coefficient µ ≈ 0.4 — significantly lower than uncoated carbide (µ ≈ 0.7). This reduces the heat generated at the chip-edge interface when cutting hardened material, where thermal management is the primary challenge. TiAlN's smooth surface also resists built-up edge formation on sticky alloy steels.
◉
Visual Wear Indicator
The characteristic gold colour of TiAlN acts as a built-in wear indicator. When the gold film wears away from the cutting edge, the silver-grey substrate becomes visible — a clear signal that the insert has reached end-of-life and must be indexed or replaced before edge collapse damages the workpiece.
⬡
Ultra-Fine WC-Co Substrate
Grain size <0.5 µm. Hardness ≈ 1700 HV. The extremely fine grain structure enables a sharp, consistent edge geometry after grinding — essential for finish milling of hardened surfaces where edge micro-chipping causes surface finish failure before bulk tool wear.

⚠ Avoid flood coolant on hardened materials — thermal shock from sudden cooling causes micro-cracking in both the workpiece surface and the TiAlN coating. Use MQL (minimum quantity lubrication) or dry milling only.
⚠ ap in hardened steel is intentionally very low. Deep cuts in hardened material generate excessive radial forces that cause edge chipping. Build up depth gradually.
⚠ Start at the lower Vc bound. TiAlN degrades above 600°C — if chips begin to show blue/violet heat colours, reduce Vc immediately by 15%.
⚠ Hardened Material Milling — Critical Requirements
✓Machine spindle rigidity: BT40 or BT50. HSK-A63 preferred. Avoid ER collet adapters.
✓Workholding: hydraulic or mechanical fixture. Vibration < 2 µm at spindle nose.
✓Climb milling (conventional milling causes edge micro-chipping in hardened work).
✓Radial runout: max 0.005 mm. Check with dial gauge before each run.
✗No flood coolant — thermal shock destroys TiAlN coating and causes surface cracking.
✗No interrupted heavy passes — ap must not exceed 0.5 mm in H-group material.
✗Never ramp or plunge into hardened material — axial entry force will fracture insert.
✗Do not run vibrating or chattering. Stop and fix resonance before resuming.

Application Guide — Hardened Steel Milling
  • ⬛
    Face Milling — Finish Pass

    Primary application. Flat surface finishing of hardened die and tool steel. Max ap 0.3–0.5 mm, ae 60–75% of cutter diameter. Produces Ra 0.4–0.8 µm surface finish when setup rigidity is adequate. Replace insert at first sign of TiAlN colour loss from the cutting edge.

  • 📐
    Shoulder Milling

    90° walls in hardened pockets and cavities. Keep ae ≤ 8 mm and ap ≤ 0.3 mm for H-group material. Wall perpendicularity improves with smaller ae — do not push radial engagement at the expense of finish quality. Use climb milling direction without exception.

  • 🔩
    Die & Mould Finish Work

    Semi-finish to finish of P20, H13, and D2 tool steel cavities and cores (50–62 HRC). Reduce both Vc and fz by 15–20% compared to the table values for curved 3D surfaces where effective cutting speed varies with tool path orientation. Program entry and exit as arcing moves, not straight plunges.

Machine Setup Checklist
  • ✓Verified spindle runout ≤ 0.005 mm with dial gauge
  • ✓Insert torqued to 2.0–2.5 Nm with calibrated wrench
  • ✓Workpiece hardness confirmed (Rockwell test)
  • ✓Climb milling direction programmed in CAM
  • ✓Flood coolant OFF — MQL or dry confirmed
  • ✓ap ≤ 0.5 mm in G-code verified before run
  • ✗Run if spindle shows chatter or resonance
  • ✗Run without first test-cutting at minimum Vc
TiAlN Wear Monitoring: The gold TiAlN colour is your wear indicator. After each pass, visually inspect the cutting edge under good lighting. When the gold film disappears from the tip or along the flank, index or replace immediately — running an exposed carbide substrate on hardened material causes catastrophic edge collapse within seconds and can damage the workpiece surface irreparably.
